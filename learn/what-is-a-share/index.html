What is a Share?

3 mins readLevel: Beginner

Understand the idea

A share is one unit of ownership in a company. When you own a share, you own a very small part of that business. The size of that part depends on how many shares the company has issued and how many you own.

Imagine this: A pizza is cut into eight equal slices. One slice represents one-eighth of the pizza. A company can also divide its ownership into many smaller units called shares. The comparison helps explain the idea of parts, although a company is not literally divided like a pizza.

What does owning a share mean?

If you buy a share of a listed company, you become one of its shareholders. You may have rights attached to that share, such as voting rights, depending on the class of share and applicable rules. Share ownership does not mean you own a particular desk, machine or product made by the company. It means you hold a part of the company's equity.

A simple example

Suppose a fictional company has issued 1,000 equal shares. If you own 10 of them, you hold 1% of those issued shares. If the company later performs well, investors may be willing to pay more for its shares. If its outlook weakens, buyers may offer less. The market price can move for many reasons, and it can fall as well as rise.

How do people buy and sell shares?

In India, investors generally place orders through a registered stockbroker. The order is sent to a stock exchange, where it can be matched with a compatible order. Shares held electronically are recorded in a demat account. The exchange, clearing corporation, broker and depository systems each have a role in completing the transaction.

What should you remember?

  • A share represents a unit of ownership in a company.
  • Shareholders are not guaranteed a profit or a dividend.
  • The market price can change, and you can lose money.
  • Owning shares is different from depositing money in a bank account.
