Mutual Funds

3 mins readLevel: Beginner

Understand the idea

A mutual fund collects money from many investors and uses that pooled money to buy a portfolio of investments, such as shares, bonds or a mix of both. Each investor owns units in the fund. The value of those units changes as the investments in the portfolio change.

Imagine This: A group of friends contribute money to buy a basket containing many different fruits. Each friend owns a portion of the basket. A mutual fund works in a similar way, except the pooled money is invested in financial assets.

A professional fund manager follows the fund's stated investment objective. For example, an equity fund mainly invests in shares, while a debt fund mainly invests in debt instruments. An index fund aims to follow a market index rather than select investments in the same active way.
