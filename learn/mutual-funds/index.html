Mutual Funds

3 mins readLevel: Beginner

Understand the idea

A mutual fund pools money from many investors and invests it according to a stated objective. The portfolio may hold shares, bonds, money-market instruments or a mix. Investors own units in the scheme; the value of those units changes with the value of the underlying portfolio and applicable costs.

Imagine several people contributing to one basket of investments. Each person owns a proportionate number of units in the fund rather than choosing and holding every underlying security directly.

How does a mutual fund work?

  1. Investors put money into a scheme.
  2. The fund deploys the pooled money according to its investment objective and stated strategy.
  3. Investors are allotted units based on the applicable Net Asset Value (NAV) and transaction rules.
  4. The portfolio value changes as its investments rise or fall, and the scheme's NAV reflects the value of its assets after liabilities and expenses.

Common types of mutual funds

  • Equity funds: invest primarily in shares and are exposed to stock-market risk.
  • Debt funds: invest mainly in debt and money-market instruments; they still carry risks such as interest-rate and credit risk.
  • Hybrid funds: combine asset classes, often including equity and debt.
  • Index funds: aim to track a stated market index, subject to tracking difference and costs.

What should a beginner understand?

NAV is the per-unit value of a scheme. The expense ratio represents recurring operating expenses charged to the scheme and affects returns. A fund manager or index strategy follows the scheme's stated mandate; neither removes investment risk.

Mutual fund returns are not guaranteed. The value can fall, and different schemes have different risks, costs, objectives and time horizons. Read the scheme information and risk disclosures before making a decision.

An SIP is one way to invest regularly in a mutual fund; it is not a separate kind of fund. Continue with how SIPs work, use the SIP calculator, and explore the Mutual Funds and SIP guide.
