Taxes on Stock Market Investments

3 mins readLevel: Intermediate

Understand the idea

When you invest in shares or mutual funds, some income or profits may be taxable. The tax treatment depends on the type of income, the investment, how long it was held and the tax rules that apply for that financial year.

For example, a profit made when you sell an investment may be treated as a capital gain. Dividends received from shares or mutual funds may be treated as income. These are different types of income and may have different tax treatment.

Imagine you buy shares and later sell them for more than you paid. The difference may be a capital gain. If the company also pays you a dividend while you hold the shares, that dividend is a separate receipt. Your tax calculation may need to consider both.
