What is NSE?

2 mins readLevel: Beginner

Understand the idea

NSE stands for the National Stock Exchange of India. It is a stock exchange: a market institution that provides an electronic platform and rule-based process for trading listed securities.

What does NSE do?

Investors do not normally walk into the exchange to buy shares. They place orders through a stockbroker. The broker routes eligible orders to the exchange's trading system, where buy and sell orders can be matched according to the exchange's rules.

NSE also provides listing and market-related services and works with clearing and settlement infrastructure. These functions help the market operate in an organised way.

When did NSE begin?

NSE was incorporated in 1992, recognised as a stock exchange in 1993 and began operations in 1994. It introduced electronic or screen-based trading in India. NSE's official website provides its history and current market information.

Is NSE the same as NIFTY 50?

No. NSE is an exchange. NIFTY 50 is a stock-market index that tracks a selected group of 50 companies listed on NSE, subject to the index methodology. An exchange is a marketplace; an index is a measure used to represent the performance of a group of securities.

Is NSE the same as a stockbroker?

No. The exchange operates the marketplace. A broker provides investors with access to that marketplace and other permitted services. The two have different roles.
