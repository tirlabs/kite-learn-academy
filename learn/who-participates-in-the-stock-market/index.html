Who Participates in the Stock Market?

2 mins readLevel: Beginner

Understand the idea

A stock market works because different participants perform different jobs. Some raise money, some invest, some provide access to trading, and others operate or regulate the market infrastructure.

1. Companies and other issuers

Companies may issue shares or other securities to raise capital. In the primary market, investors subscribe to an issue according to its terms.

2. Retail investors

Retail investors are individuals who invest or trade using their own money. Their goals, knowledge, time horizons and risk capacity can differ widely.

3. Institutional investors

Institutional investors include organisations such as mutual funds, insurance companies, pension funds and other regulated institutions. They may invest on behalf of clients, policyholders or beneficiaries, depending on their mandate.

4. Stockbrokers

Brokers provide clients with access to exchanges and may offer related services. Investors generally place orders through a broker rather than directly on the exchange's trading system.

5. Stock exchanges

Exchanges operate trading platforms and rules through which orders can be matched. NSE and BSE are two important exchanges in India.

6. Depositories and depository participants

Depositories maintain securities in electronic form. Investors access depository services through a depository participant. This is part of the system that supports demat accounts.

7. Clearing corporations and regulators

Clearing corporations support the clearing and settlement of trades. SEBI regulates the securities market and works to protect investors and promote its development.

These roles are connected but not interchangeable. For example, a broker is not the exchange, and SEBI is not a trading platform. See the Stock Market Basics guide for the full picture.
