How Shares are Bought and Sold

2 mins readLevel: Beginner

Understand the idea

Buying or selling a share involves several connected systems. The process may happen quickly on screen, but each participant has a specific role.

Step 1: The investor places an order

An investor chooses a security, quantity and order type in the broker's app or platform. A market order and a limit order behave differently: a market order seeks execution at the best available prices, while a limit order sets the price boundary at which the investor is willing to trade. Execution is not guaranteed in every situation.

Step 2: The broker sends the order to an exchange

The broker provides access to the exchange and routes the order into the relevant trading system. The exchange's system checks and processes the order under its rules.

Step 3: A compatible order is matched

A trade can happen when a buy order and a sell order are compatible on price and quantity. The price at which orders meet is determined by the order book and exchange rules, not by the broker choosing a price for the investor.

Step 4: Clearing and settlement take place

After a trade is executed, clearing and settlement processes work out the obligations of the parties and arrange the transfer of securities and funds. The exact operational details and timelines depend on the market rules in force.

Step 5: Holdings and funds are updated

When settlement is completed, securities are reflected in the buyer's demat account and the corresponding funds are transferred through the settlement system. Investors should check their broker and depository records rather than relying only on an order-screen message.

Remember

  • Placing an order does not always mean it has been executed.
  • An executed trade still goes through settlement.
  • Brokerage, taxes and other charges may apply.
  • Share prices can move against you; a trade does not guarantee a profit.

To understand the institutions involved, read about stockbrokers, demat accounts, and the Orders and Trading guide.
