What is SEBI?

2 mins readLevel: Beginner

Understand the idea

SEBI stands for the Securities and Exchange Board of India. It is the regulator of India's securities market. Its central responsibilities include protecting investors, promoting the development of the securities market and regulating that market.

Why does the stock market need a regulator?

A securities market involves companies raising money, investors taking risks and many institutions handling transactions and information. Rules help establish standards for fair conduct, disclosures, market integrity and investor protection. A regulator supervises the market framework and can act when laws or regulations are breached.

What does SEBI do?

  • Frames and administers regulations for the securities market within its legal powers.
  • Regulates stock exchanges and various market intermediaries.
  • Works to protect the interests of investors in securities.
  • Promotes the development and orderly functioning of the securities market.
  • Addresses market misconduct and investor complaints through its established processes.

Is SEBI the same as NSE or BSE?

No. SEBI is the regulator. NSE and BSE are stock exchanges that operate marketplaces under the regulatory framework. A stockbroker is a separate intermediary that provides investors access to market services.

SEBI was constituted in 1988 and became a statutory body under the SEBI Act, 1992. You can read about its mandate on SEBI's official About page.
