Long-Term vs Short-Term Investing

3 mins readLevel: Intermediate

Understand the idea

Short-term investing generally involves a shorter holding period, while long-term investing means holding investments for several years or longer. The right time horizon depends on when you need the money and how much uncertainty you can tolerate.

Imagine This: Money needed for a school fee next month has a very different purpose from money being set aside for a goal twenty years away. The time available to reach a goal should influence how the money is invested.

Short-term prices can move sharply because of news, business results, economic changes and investor expectations. Long-term investors may focus more on the quality and progress of a business, but they still face the risk of losses. A longer holding period does not guarantee a positive return.
