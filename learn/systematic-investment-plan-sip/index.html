Systematic Investment Plan (SIP)

3 mins readLevel: Beginner

Understand the idea

A Systematic Investment Plan (SIP) is a method of investing a fixed amount at regular intervals, often monthly, into a mutual fund. It describes the way contributions are made; it is not a separate investment product and it does not guarantee a profit.

Suppose an investor contributes ₹2,000 each month. The amount buys units at the applicable NAV for each instalment. When NAV is lower, the same contribution buys more units; when NAV is higher, it buys fewer. The total units accumulated depend on the contribution amounts and the applicable NAVs.

Why do people use SIPs?

  • They create a regular investing routine.
  • They allow contributions to be spread over time rather than requiring one large initial amount.
  • They can help an investor follow a planned contribution schedule.

What is rupee-cost averaging?

Because a fixed contribution buys different numbers of units at different NAVs, the average purchase cost per unit may be spread across market levels. This is often called rupee-cost averaging. It does not prevent losses, ensure a profit or guarantee a better outcome than investing a lump sum.

SIP versus lump sum

With an SIP, money is invested in instalments. With a lump-sum investment, a larger amount is invested at one time. The outcomes depend on market movements, timing, fund choice, costs and the investor's circumstances. Neither method guarantees a better return.
