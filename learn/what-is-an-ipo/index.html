What is an IPO?

3 mins readLevel: Intermediate

Understand the idea

An Initial Public Offering (IPO) is when a company offers its shares to the public for the first time and seeks to list them on a stock exchange. After listing, the shares can generally be traded by investors on the exchange.

Imagine This: A growing family-owned business wants to raise money to expand. It decides to offer shares to public investors and become a listed company. This process can give the business access to a wider pool of investors.

An IPO may include a fresh issue, an offer for sale by existing shareholders, or both. In a fresh issue, the company issues new shares and receives the proceeds, subject to the offer terms. In an offer for sale, existing shareholders sell some of their shares and receive the proceeds.
