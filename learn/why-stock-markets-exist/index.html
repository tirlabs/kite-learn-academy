Stock Market Basics

Why Stock Markets Exist

2 mins readLevel: Beginner

Understand the idea

A stock market brings buyers and sellers of securities together through an organised system. It helps companies raise money by issuing securities and gives investors a place to trade securities that have already been issued.

Imagine this: Farmers bring produce to a marketplace so buyers can compare what is available and make transactions in one organised place. A stock exchange performs a related role for securities, using electronic systems and market rules rather than physical stalls.

Why do companies issue shares?

A company may need money to expand, build facilities, develop products, repay debt or support other business plans. One way it can raise capital is by issuing shares to investors. When shares are offered to the public for the first time, the process may be an initial public offering, or IPO.

Primary market and secondary market

In the primary market, a company or other issuer offers new securities to investors. The issuer receives the proceeds from a fresh issue, subject to the terms of the offer.

In the secondary market, investors buy and sell securities that have already been issued. When one investor sells an existing share to another investor on an exchange, the company does not normally receive the sale proceeds from that trade.

Why is a marketplace useful?

  • Access: Investors can place orders through brokers connected to an exchange.
  • Price discovery: Buy and sell orders help establish the prices at which trades take place.
  • Liquidity: Investors may be able to sell a security rather than having to find a buyer privately. Liquidity is not guaranteed for every security or at every moment.
  • Rules and oversight: Exchanges operate under a regulatory framework, with SEBI overseeing the securities market in India.

Does the stock exchange decide the share price?

No single exchange employee chooses the price of each share. Prices emerge from the interaction of buy and sell orders. If buyers are willing to pay more and sellers are willing to accept those prices, trades may occur at higher prices. If demand weakens or sellers accept lower prices, trades may occur lower.
