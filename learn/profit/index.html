Profit

3 mins readLevel: Intermediate

Understand the idea

Profit is what remains after a business subtracts its expenses from the revenue it earns. Expenses can include the cost of materials, employee salaries, rent, interest, depreciation and taxes. Profit helps us understand whether the business is earning more than it spends.

Imagine This: A café earns ₹1,00,000 in a month. It spends ₹80,000 on ingredients, salaries, rent and other costs. The remaining ₹20,000 is its profit for that period, before considering any other items not included in the example.

Companies report different profit measures. Operating profit focuses on the result from business operations before certain non-operating items. Net profit is the amount left after the expenses and other items included in the income statement, such as interest and tax.
