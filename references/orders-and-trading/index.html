Orders and Trading: A Beginner's Guide

Understand market orders, limit orders, the order book and the difference between investing and intraday trading.

An order is an instruction

An order tells your broker what you want to buy or sell, how much, and under what conditions. An order is not the same as a completed trade.

Market orders

A market order prioritises execution at the best available prices. The exact execution price can vary, especially when prices move quickly or a security is thinly traded.

Limit orders

A limit order sets the maximum price you are willing to pay when buying, or the minimum price you are willing to accept when selling. It may not execute if the market does not reach your limit.

Order book and bid-ask spread

The order book shows available buy and sell orders. The difference between the best ask and best bid is the bid-ask spread; a wider spread can increase trading costs.

Before placing an order

Check the security, quantity, order type, price, product type, estimated charges and risk. Learn your broker's rules for modification, cancellation and execution.

Educational information only. This guide is for understanding concepts, not a recommendation to buy or sell any security. Market rules, charges and tax treatment can change; verify current information with official sources and your broker.
