Orders and Trading: A Beginner's Guide

Understand market orders, limit orders, the order book and the difference between investing and intraday trading.

From an idea to an order

When an investor decides to buy or sell a listed share, the instruction is entered through a registered broker's trading platform. The order includes the security, quantity and conditions such as order type and price. Placing an order does not mean it has been executed.

The roles of the accounts and intermediaries

A bank account is used for money. A trading account is used to place orders through a broker. A demat account holds securities in electronic form. The broker provides access to the exchange; the exchange's trading system matches eligible buy and sell orders. Learn more about stock brokers and demat accounts.

Market orders

A market order seeks execution at the best available prices. The final price is not guaranteed and can differ from the price last displayed, especially when markets move quickly or liquidity is limited.

Limit orders

A limit order sets the maximum price a buyer is willing to pay or the minimum price a seller is willing to accept. It controls the acceptable price, but execution is not guaranteed: the market may never reach the limit or there may not be enough matching quantity.

Bid, ask and the order book

The bid is the price buyers are currently offering; the ask is the price sellers are currently asking. The difference is the bid–ask spread. The order book displays available orders at different prices. A wide spread or low depth can make execution less predictable.

What happens when orders match?

When compatible buy and sell orders meet under the exchange's matching rules, a trade is executed. Clearing and settlement then arrange the exchange of funds and securities. For the normal equity cash market, NSE describes a T+1 rolling settlement cycle, while an optional T+0 cycle is also available for eligible securities and participants. Actual settlement treatment depends on the segment, security and applicable rules. See NSE's settlement-cycle information.

Before placing an order

  • Check the company/security name and symbol carefully.
  • Confirm buy or sell, quantity and order type.
  • Understand price limits, validity and product type.
  • Review estimated charges and available funds/holdings.
  • Know how your broker handles modification, cancellation and partial execution.

Investing and intraday trading are not the same

Delivery investing generally involves taking delivery of securities, while intraday trading involves positions intended to be opened and closed within the same trading session under the broker's product rules. Intraday trading can involve substantial risk, and broker product labels and square-off rules vary.

Educational information only. This guide explains market mechanics; it is not a recommendation to trade. Order types, charges, settlement rules and tax treatment can change. Verify current details with the exchange, regulator and your broker.
