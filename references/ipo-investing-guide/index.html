IPO Investing Guide for Beginners

Understand IPOs, offer documents, ASBA, UPI mandates, allotment and listing in India.

What is an IPO?

An Initial Public Offering is when a company offers securities to public investors and seeks admission to trading on a stock exchange. The offer document explains the issue, the company, its financial information, intended use of proceeds and important risks. An IPO is not a promise of listing gains.

Fresh issue and offer for sale

In a fresh issue, the company issues new shares and receives the proceeds, subject to the stated terms. In an offer for sale, existing shareholders sell some of their shares and receive the proceeds. An offer can include both components. Read the offer document to understand how much of the issue is fresh issue and how much is an offer for sale.

Before you apply

  • Read the offer document and understand the company's business, financial position, risks and use of proceeds.
  • Check the issue dates, price band, lot size, permitted investor category and application instructions in the current issue documents.
  • Use only the official channels and intermediaries identified by the regulator, exchange, issuer or registered intermediary.
  • Never share your UPI PIN, OTP, password or approve an unexpected collect request.

How an application works

Investors submit bid details through a permitted application channel. The application may use ASBA, which stands for Application Supported by Blocked Amount. Under ASBA, the application amount is blocked in the investor's bank account and is not transferred to the issuer at the time of application. If shares are allotted, the amount payable is debited; otherwise, the blocked amount is released.

How the UPI mandate fits in

For eligible individual applications using UPI, the application includes the investor's UPI ID. A mandate request is sent through the relevant banking/payment infrastructure. The investor should verify the details in the mandate before authorising it with their UPI PIN. A successful mandate blocks the amount; it does not mean shares have been allotted.

SEBI explains the broad process in its ASBA investor guidance and UPI IPO application guidance. The available modes, thresholds and operational details can change, so check the current issue instructions and SEBI's latest requirements.

What happens after the issue closes?

After bidding closes, applications are reconciled and the basis of allotment is finalised under the applicable process. An applicant may receive all, some or none of the shares applied for. Where shares are allotted, the amount due is debited and shares are credited to the investor's demat account according to the issue schedule. Any excess blocked amount is released. The precise timeline is issue-specific and should be checked in the official schedule.

If you receive no shares

If no shares are allotted to your application, the application amount should not be used to pay for shares. Under ASBA, the blocked amount is released; for an eligible UPI-based application, the block is revoked through the applicable process. You do not receive shares in your demat account for that application. If only some of the shares you applied for are allotted, the amount due for the allotted shares is debited and the remaining blocked amount is released.

The release is not necessarily visible at the exact moment the allotment result appears. Check the official allotment status and the issue's published schedule, then review the relevant bank account or UPI-linked account. If the block remains after the stated release timeline, contact your bank or the intermediary through which you applied, and use the official issue/registrar contact details where appropriate. Keep your application number and mandate reference available. Do not share your UPI PIN, OTP or banking password with anyone claiming to help release the funds.

What does listing mean?

Listing is the admission of the company's shares to trading on the exchange. Once trading begins, the market price is determined by buy and sell orders. It can be higher or lower than the issue price. Investors should not assume that every IPO will list at a premium or produce a profit.

Common misunderstandings

  • “A UPI mandate means I got the shares.” No. It only authorises blocking of funds; allotment is a separate process.
  • “No allotment means my money is lost.” Under ASBA, funds for shares not allotted are unblocked; check your bank and application status if the release is delayed.
  • “A famous company is automatically a good IPO.” Brand recognition does not replace analysis of valuation, financials, risks and offer terms.
  • “Listing gains are certain.” No. Market prices can move in either direction.
