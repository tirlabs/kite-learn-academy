Investment Risk and Portfolio Building

Learn how risk, diversification, asset allocation and position sizing fit together.

Risk is more than a falling price

Investment risk is the possibility that an outcome differs from what an investor expects. It includes losing money, not having access to money when needed, holding too much in one asset, inflation reducing purchasing power and an investment failing to meet a goal.

Volatility and permanent loss

Volatility describes how much prices move over time. A volatile investment can rise and fall sharply. Volatility is one kind of risk, but it is not the only one: business failure, excessive debt, fraud, poor liquidity and paying an unjustified price can also harm investors.

Diversification

Diversification means spreading exposure across different investments, companies, sectors or asset classes. It can reduce the impact of one holding performing badly. It cannot eliminate market-wide losses, guarantee returns or make an unsuitable investment safe.

Asset allocation and time horizon

Asset allocation is the mix of broad asset classes in a portfolio. The appropriate mix depends on the purpose of the money, when it may be needed, liquidity requirements and the investor's ability and willingness to tolerate losses. There is no single allocation that is right for everyone.

Position sizing and concentration

Position sizing is the amount of a portfolio exposed to one investment. A concentrated position can make overall results depend heavily on one company or theme. Understanding position size helps make that exposure visible; it does not remove the risk of loss.

Review the whole portfolio

Look beyond the number of holdings. Several funds may own many of the same companies, creating overlap. Review concentration, costs, liquidity, risk, time horizon and whether the portfolio still serves its stated purpose. Avoid changing a long-term plan solely in response to short-term market noise.

A simple example

If a portfolio holds only shares of one company, a serious problem at that company can affect nearly the entire portfolio. Holding a broader mix may reduce the impact of that one event, but a broad market decline can still affect many holdings at once.

Educational information only. This guide does not recommend a personal asset allocation or specific investment. Consider your own circumstances and seek qualified advice when needed.
