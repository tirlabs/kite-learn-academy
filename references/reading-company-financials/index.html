Reading Company Financials: A Starting Point

A beginner-friendly map to the income statement, balance sheet, cash flow and common financial ratios.

Three connected statements

The income statement reports financial performance over a period. The balance sheet shows assets, liabilities and equity at a point in time. The cash flow statement reports cash movements during a period.

Revenue and profit

Revenue indicates sales generated. Expenses are deducted to arrive at profit measures. Rising revenue does not guarantee rising profit, and reported profit does not necessarily mean cash has been collected.

Assets and liabilities

Assets are resources controlled by a company; liabilities are obligations. Equity represents the residual interest after liabilities are deducted from assets.

Cash flow matters

Cash flow helps assess how a business generates and uses cash. Compare operating cash flow with reported profit over time and investigate persistent differences.

Ratios need context

EPS, P/E, ROE and ROCE can help frame questions, but no single ratio is enough. Compare periods, peers, business models and the assumptions behind the numbers.

Educational information only. This guide is for understanding concepts, not a recommendation to buy or sell any security. Market rules, charges and tax treatment can change; verify current information with official sources and your broker.
