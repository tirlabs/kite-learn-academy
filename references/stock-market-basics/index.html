Stock Market Basics: How the Market Works

A practical overview of shares, exchanges, brokers and how an order becomes a trade.

What the stock market is

A stock market provides a regulated venue and process for buying and selling securities. A share represents ownership in a company; its traded price changes as buyers and sellers interact.

Primary and secondary markets

In the primary market, a company issues securities to raise capital, such as through an IPO. In the secondary market, investors trade securities that have already been issued.

The main participants

Companies issue securities. Investors and traders place orders. Brokers provide market access. Exchanges operate trading platforms, while regulators set and enforce market rules.

How a trade happens

An investor places an order through a broker. The order is sent to the exchange, where it may match with a compatible order. Clearing and settlement processes then complete the transaction.

What to understand first

Learn what a share represents, how demat and trading accounts differ, how orders work, and why prices move before risking money.

Educational information only. This guide is for understanding concepts, not a recommendation to buy or sell any security. Market rules, charges and tax treatment can change; verify current information with official sources and your broker.
