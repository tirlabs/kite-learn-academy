Position sizing starts with a maximum rupee amount you are prepared to risk on a trade. Dividing that risk budget by the entry-to-stop distance estimates a share quantity.

How is this calculated?

Translate a risk budget into a share quantity. The calculation uses the following method:

Risk budget = Capital × risk percentage; quantity = floor(risk budget ÷ (entry − stop)).

Worked example

With ₹1,00,000 capital and 1% risk, the risk budget is ₹1,000. If entry is ₹250 and stop is ₹240, the distance is ₹10 per share, giving an illustrative size of 100 shares before costs.

Assumptions and limitations

This is a planning calculation, not a recommendation. It assumes a long trade, stop below entry and execution at the entered prices. Gaps, slippage, charges and liquidity can increase actual loss.

Use the inputs as an illustration, not as a prediction or recommendation. Results are calculated in your browser and are not sent to KiteLearn.
