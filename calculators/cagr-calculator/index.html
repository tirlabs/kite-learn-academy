CAGR is the constant annual growth rate that would connect a starting value to an ending value over a stated period. It smooths the path into one comparable annual rate; it does not show the ups and downs along the way.

How is this calculated?

Understand annualised growth. The calculation uses the following method:

CAGR = [(Ending value ÷ Starting value)1 ÷ years − 1] × 100

Worked example

If ₹1,00,000 becomes ₹2,00,000 in 5 years, CAGR is about 14.87% per year. The actual year-by-year returns may have varied considerably.

Assumptions and limitations

Both values must be positive and the period must be greater than zero. CAGR ignores interim cash flows, volatility and the path taken between the two values.

Use the inputs as an illustration, not as a prediction or recommendation. Results are calculated in your browser and are not sent to KiteLearn.
