A lumpsum is a single amount invested at one point in time. Unlike a SIP, no further contributions are assumed. The estimate shows how the starting amount may grow when returns are reinvested.

How is this calculated?

How a one-time investment compounds. The calculation uses the following method:

Future value = Initial investment × (1 + annual return)years

Worked example

For ₹1,00,000 invested for 10 years at an assumed 12% annual return, annual compounding gives an illustrative future value of about ₹3,10,585. The gain is the future value minus the original investment.

Assumptions and limitations

This model assumes the entire amount is invested at the beginning and compounds once each year. Actual market returns are uneven; taxes, fees, inflation and timing are excluded.

Use the inputs as an illustration, not as a prediction or recommendation. Results are calculated in your browser and are not sent to KiteLearn.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between SIP and lumpsum?

A lumpsum invests the full amount at once. A SIP spreads contributions over time, so the timing of each investment differs.

Does this predict mutual fund returns?

No. The annual return is an input for illustration, not a forecast or promise.
