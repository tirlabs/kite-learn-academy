A risk-reward ratio compares the amount at risk between entry and stop-loss with the potential gain between entry and target. It describes a trade plan; it does not estimate the probability of success.

How is this calculated?

Compare planned downside with potential upside. The calculation uses the following method:

Potential risk = (Entry − Stop) × quantity; potential reward = (Target − Entry) × quantity; ratio = reward ÷ risk.

Worked example

For an entry at ₹100, stop at ₹95 and target at ₹115, the potential reward is ₹15 per share against ₹5 risk, a 3:1 reward-to-risk ratio.

Assumptions and limitations

This version models a long position with stop below entry and target above entry. It excludes gaps, slippage, brokerage, taxes and execution risk. A favourable ratio does not guarantee a profitable trade.

Use the inputs as an illustration, not as a prediction or recommendation. Results are calculated in your browser and are not sent to KiteLearn.
